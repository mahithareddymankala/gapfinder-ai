SUBJECT: MACHINE LEARNING
SAMPLE STUDY MATERIAL FOR GAPFINDER AI

CHAPTER 1: SUPERVISED LEARNING

Supervised learning is a machine learning technique in which a model learns from labeled training data. Each training example contains input features and a known target output.

Classification predicts discrete categories. Examples include spam detection, sentiment classification, and disease classification.

Regression predicts continuous numerical values. Examples include house-price prediction, temperature prediction, and sales forecasting.

CHAPTER 2: OVERFITTING AND UNDERFITTING

Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too closely, including noise and irrelevant patterns. It may achieve high training accuracy but perform poorly on unseen data.

Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to learn the underlying patterns in the data. It performs poorly on both training and test data.

Overfitting can be reduced through cross-validation, regularization, feature selection, and simpler models.

CHAPTER 3: MODEL EVALUATION

A training dataset is used to fit a model. A test dataset evaluates how well the model generalizes to unseen examples.

Accuracy is the proportion of all predictions that are correct.

Precision is the proportion of predicted positive cases that are actually positive.

Recall is the proportion of actual positive cases correctly identified by the model.

The F1-score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It can be useful when balancing these two metrics, particularly for imbalanced classification problems.

CHAPTER 4: DATA LEAKAGE

Data leakage occurs when information that would not be available at prediction time is used during model training or evaluation.

Leakage can lead to misleadingly high evaluation scores and poor real-world performance.

To reduce leakage, split data appropriately and fit preprocessing steps using the training dataset only. Apply the fitted transformations to validation and test data.
